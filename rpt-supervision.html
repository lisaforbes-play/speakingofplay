My Approach to Supervision

Supervision should be a place to grow, not perform.

I believe supervision is most meaningful when clinicians feel supported enough to be curious, honest, reflective, and willing to explore the places where they feel uncertain.

My supervision approach is grounded in Child-Centered Play Therapy and informed by a narrative lens. I'm interested in helping clinicians notice what is happening in the child's play, understand the unfolding therapeutic process, and develop greater intentionality without becoming unnecessarily directive.

We'll explore not only what you do in the playroom, but what you're noticing, wondering, feeling, and learning as a therapist.

Clinical Confidence

Strengthen your ability to recognize therapeutic movement, respond to challenges, and trust the process without feeling pressure to force change.

Theoretical Depth

Move beyond simply knowing play therapy techniques toward understanding the theory, relationships, and mechanisms that inform your clinical choices.

Reflective Practice

Explore your own responses, assumptions, strengths, and growing edges with curiosity rather than judgment.

Working Toward Your RPT

Supervision for Registered Play Therapist Credentialing

The Registered Play Therapist (RPT) credential is awarded by the Association for Play Therapy (APT). Applicants must meet APT's education, clinical experience, training, and supervision requirements.

As a Registered Play Therapist Supervisor (RPT-S), I offer supervision intended to support clinicians pursuing this credential. Individual and group supervision hours are subject to APT's current requirements and eligibility rules.

Before beginning, we'll discuss your current credentialing progress and the type of supervision that best fits your needs.
