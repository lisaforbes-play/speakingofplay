Narrative Child-Centered Play Therapy

Play is not just expression. It is embodied storytelling.

Narrative Child-Centered Play Therapy is a narrative-informed extension of child-centered play therapy that understands children’s play as an embodied storytelling process in which tensions, meanings, identities, relationships, and new possibilities can emerge through the child’s own play.

Central premise

The child is already telling the story.

The therapist does not need to impose a narrative, interpret symbols from an adult position, or direct the child toward a preferred ending. The work begins with learning to notice and witness the story already becoming visible through play.

The therapist witnesses play as an embodied storytelling process in which new possibilities emerge through the child’s own play.

What makes this different

Presence before interpretation
Attend closely without rushing to explain the play from an adult framework.
Story before outcome
Trust movement emerging from the child’s own unfolding play rather than pushing for resolution.
Possibility rather than correction
Notice when the play begins to make room for something new rather than replacing a “bad” story with a “better” one.
Theoretical foundations

Two traditions in conversation.

  • Child-centered play therapy and trust in the child’s self-directed process
  • Narrative understandings of story, meaning, identity, and possibility
  • Play as lived, embodied communication rather than a puzzle to decode
  • A relational stance emphasizing witnessing, attunement, and curiosity
The role of the therapist

Notice more. Rush less.

The therapist stays close to repetition, tension, shifts, relationships, and emerging possibilities in the play. Questions or reflections are used carefully and only when they deepen what the child has already communicated through the unfolding story.
