Narrative Child-Centered Play Therapy

Play is not just expression. It is embodied storytelling.

Narrative Child-Centered Play Therapy is a way of witnessing spontaneous play as embodied storytelling and attending to how children’s lived stories repeat, organize, and gradually expand within a consistent child-centered relationship.

The therapist witnesses play as an embodied storytelling process in which new possibilities emerge through the child’s own play.
What makes this different

A different way of seeing the same child-centered process.

The model does not ask the therapist to become more directive. It asks the therapist to become more intentional about attention, timing, and pattern recognition.

01

Presence before interpretation

Stay close to what is happening without rushing to explain the play from an adult framework.

02

Story before outcome

Trust the child’s unfolding process instead of pushing the play toward a therapeutic resolution.

03

Possibility rather than correction

Notice when a previously unavailable role, relationship, action, or outcome begins to appear in the play.

Theoretical foundations

Two traditions in conversation.

This approach remains fully grounded in a non-directive, child-centered stance while adding a narrative lens for recognizing how experience organizes into story over time.

  • Child-centered trust in the child’s self-directed process
  • Narrative attention to story, meaning, identity, and possibility
  • Play as lived, embodied communication rather than a puzzle to decode
  • A relational stance built around witnessing, attunement, and curiosity
The role of the therapist

Notice more. Rush less.

The therapist follows the child and also tracks where the story seems to organize: repetition, tension, emotional weight, roles, relationships, and subtle shifts.

Questions or reflections are used carefully and only when they deepen what the child has already communicated through the play rather than introducing something from outside the story.

Four anchors for attention

What do I stay close to?

TensionWhat cannot yet comfortably coexist?
RepetitionWhat keeps happening?
Emotional weightWhere does the play become especially alive, slow, intense, or urgent?
ShiftWhat is different this time, even slightly?
